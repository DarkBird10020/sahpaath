# Natural photographic art direction

## What changed

The user rejected both miniature and sculptural CGI approaches. The new target is believable, full-size learning spaces with the visual depth needed for a future camera walkthrough. Image quality is established before animation. The images are fictional generated environments, not photographs of a real SahPaath classroom.

## Reference analysis

The privately inspected [Wright & Wright library photograph](https://www.wrightandwright.co.uk/projects/magdalen-college-oxford) shows consistent vertical lines, daylight from one side, variations in timber grain, properly supported furniture, naturally uneven book arrangements, and successive foreground/middle/background planes. These are the useful cues to reproduce in an original composition. Its historical building, layout and photograph are not copied or included in the app.

[Google's official guide](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/gemini/prompting-tips-nano-banana-pro/) recommends specifying subject, composition, action, location and style, then camera/lighting and reference roles. [Higgsfield's published prompt examples](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/Nano-Banana-Pro-Expert-Use-Cases) demonstrate explicit spatial organization. These are published guidance; the hidden original prompt of any reference image is unknown. Our prompts are inferred art briefs, not recovered originals.

## Candidate A — shared learning table

Create a natural-looking architectural interior photograph for the introductory story of an education website. It must read as a photograph of a real, usable room at human scale. Ordinary materials with subtle imperfections: pale oak showing irregular grain, off-white limewash with faint texture, matte navy fabric, clear window glass with very faint reflections. Believable furniture construction and realistic proportions. Carefully exposed daylight, neutral whites, restrained saturation, gentle contrast, natural shadow detail. Camera at seated-to-standing human eye level, 1.35 metres high, 28mm architectural lens, corrected verticals, f/8 depth of field. Foreground, middle distance and room structure remain clearly resolved. No miniature, model, diorama, clay, sculpture, gigantic book, floating objects, cutaway roof, glossy plastic, holographic interface, neon, volumetric rays, bloom, excessive HDR, orange-and-teal grading or artificial blur. No people, captions, logos, watermarks or readable educational claims. Original imagined architecture, not a recreation of any specific reference building. Wide 16:9 photograph. Scene A — The shared learning table. A quiet contemporary learning studio on a bright overcast morning. View from just inside an open timber doorway, with the near door jamb at the far left providing a gentle foreground edge. A long pale-oak shared table sits diagonally from the lower right into the middle of the room. Four simple navy upholstered chairs are pulled in naturally, one slightly angled. On the nearest part of the table: a normal-sized open blank exercise book with a gently curling page, a graphite pencil and an amber cloth bookmark; farther away a plain closed notebook and ordinary black headphones. Large tall windows along the LEFT wall provide soft directional daylight. Beyond the table, low oak bookshelves and a quiet reading alcove lead through a wide rectangular opening into a second connected learning room. A leafy courtyard is visible outside, modest and real. Ceiling height about 3.2 metres. No grand atrium or luxury-hotel styling. Composition has strong spatial depth and a clear unobstructed path into the second room. Quiet, lived-in, welcoming, editorial architecture photography.

## Review before motion

Check full-resolution output for furniture support, window and shelf alignment, readable room depth, plausible shadows, natural wood texture, excessive smoothness, accidental text and distorted objects. A sharp large image alone is not proof of realism. Inspect contact sheets and motion frames before extending a camera sequence. Future motion should preserve the photographed room and use a short forward dolly through an existing opening, with no invented object transformations.
